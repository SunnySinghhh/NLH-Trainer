How this works and where the ranges come from

No range data is loaded. Every table in ranges.js is empty, so there is nothing to drill and nothing to chart yet.

A spot is a position and an action to you — nothing more. Whether a 3-bet reached you because you opened and got raised, or because you called and two players raised behind, the right answer is the same, so the trainer does not ask. Every spot offers the same four options, including ones that are not legal: the choice is made out of the whole set rather than one already narrowed for you.

The roll. Some hands are not played the same way every time. Those cells are split in the chart, and the number rolled for each hand decides which way this one goes: fold at the bottom of 1-100, then call, then raise. A hand that calls half the time and raises half the time calls on 1-50 and raises on 51-100. Every hand gets a roll, not just the mixed ones — rolling only when it mattered would tell you a hand was mixed before you had to decide.

Assumptions baked in: 8-handed, 100bb effective, a standard ~2.5bb open, and no ante or straddle. Facing much larger opens you should defend tighter than this; facing min-raises, wider. The small blind is at the table but is never drilled, because the simulation these ranges come from does not cover it.

What is actually verified: structural checks run over all 0 spots on every build — every hand is a real starting hand, no hand's frequencies add to more than 100%, the 1-100 bands cover every roll exactly once with no gaps, aces are never folded, opening ranges widen and nest from under the gun to the button, and continuing ranges tighten from a 3-bet to a 4-bet. A check with nothing to run against reports as skipped rather than passing, so a half-filled chart never reads as green. These catch a chart that contradicts itself; they cannot tell you it is the best strategy.
